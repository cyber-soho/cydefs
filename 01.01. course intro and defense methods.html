Lesson 1 — Introduction to the Course

The competency EZ03 — apply access control measures — breaks into four elements. Notice how often the word prove appears.

ElementIn plain language
1. Apply cyberdefence techniquesKnow where the network can be entered, put controls at those points, divide the network into zones, run active defences, build in redundancy.
2. Control access to hostsKnow what each machine is for, control who can log into it and with what rights, configure its own firewall, harden it, then prove the hardening worked.
3. Control network accessControl identities and permissions across zones, configure the network firewall, deploy detection and prevention, secure wired and wireless protocols, then prove it works.
4. Apply cryptography techniquesUnderstand the basic concepts, implement the main cryptosystems, know how they are attacked, choose the right one for the job, protect data in transit and at rest.

The Line You Do Not Cross

In Canada, unauthorised use of a computer is an offence under section 342.1 of the Criminal Code. Mischief in relation to computer data is section 430(1.1). You test your own lab environment. Never the College network. Never an employer's network without a signed scope. Authorisation means written permission, from someone with the authority to grant it, covering the specific systems, during a specific window.

Lesson 2 — Defense Methods

2.1 — Three Definitions, Stated Precisely

Defence in depthMultiple independent controls along the same attack path, so one failure doesn't mean compromise.
Defensible architectureA network design that makes defence possible: named boundaries, inspection points, known system roles, and logs.
Cyber resilienceKeeping the business running while an attack is underway and after it succeeds — tested backups, procedures, spare capacity.

2.2 — The Seven Layers

LAYER 1

Governance & People

Policies, awareness, joiner-mover-leaver process

LAYER 2

Physical

Locked racks, badge access, secure disposal

LAYER 3

Perimeter

Edge firewall, published-service list, VPN gateway

LAYER 4

Network

VLAN zoning, IDS/IPS, DNS filtering, wireless separation

LAYER 5

Host

Host firewall, endpoint protection, patching, hardening

LAYER 6

Application & Identity

Least privilege, MFA, service-account hygiene

LAYER 7

Data & Recovery

Encryption, offline backup, tested restore

An attacker must defeat every layer in sequence; you only need one layer to hold.

2.3 — Finding the Doors: Attack Surface & Entry Points

Attack surface: the complete set of points where an unauthorised party could enter, extract data, or disrupt a system. Entry point: one specific such point. Attack vector: the method used at an entry point.

CategoryExamplesWhy it gets missed
Deliberate & knownVPN gateway, web server, mail flowRarely reviewed after the day they were built
Deliberate & forgottenOld firewall rule; vendor's remote-support toolNobody removed it because nobody owned it
AccidentalService bound to 0.0.0.0; public test systemDefault configuration, never audited
Not recognised as computersPrinters, cameras, smart TVs, badge readersNobody thinks of them as attack surface

2.6 — Essential Cyber Hygiene & Frameworks

CIS Controls v8.1 IG1 defines essential cyber hygiene. Priority order: 1) Know what you have · 2) Control administrators · 3) Configure securely · 4) Patch on schedule · 5) Collect logs off-host · 6) Protect the boundary/segment · 7) Back up and test restore · 8) Train the people.

FrameworkPublished byWhat it is for
NIST CSF 2.0NISTGovern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover
CIS Controls v8.1Center for Internet SecurityWhat to do first, prioritised safeguards
MITRE ATT&CK v19MITRE CorporationHow adversaries actually behave — shared vocabulary
Baseline Controls for SMOsCanadian Centre for Cyber SecurityCanadian guidance for small/medium orgs

NIST CSF tells you how to organise, CIS tells you what to do first, and ATT&CK tells you what you are defending against.

2.7 — Active Defence (and What It Is Not)

Active defence means detecting, disrupting, delaying and studying an adversary inside systems you own or are authorised to operate — IPS, automated isolation, canary tokens, rate limiting, adversary emulation. It is NOT "hacking back." Your authority ends at your own boundary, and your boundary is defined by a contract, not by a router.

2.8 — The Security Operations Centre (SOC)

TermExpansionWhat it does
SIEMSecurity Information and Event ManagementCollects logs, correlates, raises alerts

MTTD (Mean Time To Detect) and MTTR (Mean Time To Respond/Recover) are the two numbers a SOC is measured on.

2.9 — Measuring Depth: Why the Second Layer Beats the Best Layer

Residual risk = the product of each layer's chance of failing. A single excellent control (90%) still leaves far more residual risk than seven ordinary, cheap layers combined.

ScenarioResidual risk
Flat (basic firewall + AV)31.50%
Single strong (90% NGFW alone)10.00%
Defended (7 ordinary layers)0.49%

Assumes independent layer failure — always name your correlated failure modes (e.g. one stolen domain-admin credential can defeat four layers at once).
