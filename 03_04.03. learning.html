Lesson 1 — checkpoint

Five sentences to remember

  • Every access control decision has a subject, an operation and an object.
  • A permission belongs to the object; a privilege belongs to the identity.
  • Least privilege limits the level of access; need-to-know limits its scope.
  • One group per role; permissions are attached to groups only.
  • Joiner, mover, leaver; review with evidence; disable before delete.
Lesson 2 — checkpoint

Five sentences to remember

  • NTFS: rights are cumulative, Deny wins, explicit beats inherited, absence means no access, inheritance propagates; through a share, the more restrictive of share and NTFS applies.
  • Linux: owner, group, others; r = 4, w = 2, x = 1; one class decides; 2770 for a role folder; sudo rules edited with visudo only.
  • Eight techniques secure sensitive data, each attached to the process it protects; a lockout policy turns 86 400 attempts per day into 480.
  • Backdoors, Trojans, rootkits, worms, bots and botnets each need an access they should not have; detection tells you the layers are being tested.
  • Every mechanism of these two classes is applied in LAB_02 and reused in LAB_03.
