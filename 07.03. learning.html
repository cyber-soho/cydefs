Intrusion Detection & Prevention Systems

A firewall decides whether traffic may pass. It does not judge what the traffic is trying to do. This Study Hub covers the systems that fill that gap: what an IDS alerts on, what an IPS blocks, where the sensors go, and which CIS Control 13 safeguards they satisfy. Use the tabs above to memorise the vocabulary, then test your architectural judgment.

The four lessons

  • 1. IDS and IPS — definitions, the three detection methods, alert versus drop, and what a mistake costs in each mode.
  • 2. Architecture — the four IDPS components, the six-stage network sensor pipeline, and what a host agent watches.
  • 3. Placement — five candidate positions, mirror port vs tap vs inline, and the HOME_NET setting.
  • 4. CIS Controls — Control 13 safeguard by safeguard, and why detection comes after hygiene.

Quick Pro-Tip

Detection is identical in both modes — only the action differs. That is why a new sensor is almost always started in IDS mode: out-of-band, a false positive costs an analyst a few minutes; inline, the same false positive takes the web shop offline. Tune before you block.
