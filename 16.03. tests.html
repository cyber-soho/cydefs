Cryptography and Secure Communication: the terms of Class 16

This Study Hub covers the three lessons of Class 16: what a tunnel does and when a company needs one; WireGuard, OpenVPN and the built-in VPN (Virtual Private Network) features of pfSense and OPNsense; keys, users and allowed networks. Use the tabs above to learn the terms with the flashcards, then to test yourself with the practice questions.

Memorization30 flashcards
Self-assessment18 practice questions12 with one right answer, 6 with several

How to use this page

  • Flashcards: click a card to see the definition; use the arrows or the keyboard arrows to move; filter by lesson.
  • Practice test: 18 questions in the order of the lessons. A question with several right answers says how many to select, in round brackets.
  • Every option has an explanation, shown after you answer. The result page gives your score by lesson and a review of every question.
  • Nothing is saved: closing or reloading the page resets the cards and the test.

Study advice

Learn the terms first, then take the test. For every answer marked Not correct, read the explanation under each option before moving on: the reason an option is wrong is often the point of the question. This page is practice only and is not graded; Quiz 03, the graded assessment, is prepared separately. The same terms return in LAB_06 (Class 17, Saturday, October 10, 2026), in Chapter 6 of the Final Project and in Chapter 6 of the Course Procedure Manual.

What is covered

  • 1Lesson 1 · What a tunnel does and when a company needs one: private addresses, encapsulation, confidentiality, integrity, anti-replay protection, perfect forward secrecy, tunnel types, full and split tunnels, NorthMaple's decision.
  • 2Lesson 2 · WireGuard, OpenVPN, pfSense and OPNsense: fixed algorithms, cryptokey routing, control and data channels, tls-crypt, IPsec (Internet Protocol Security) with IKEv2 (Internet Key Exchange version 2), protocols to avoid, official guidance, the weighted-sum decision method.
  • 3Lesson 3 · Keys, users and allowed networks: AAA (Authentication, Authorization and Accounting), key pairs and key hygiene, access register, MFA (Multi-Factor Authentication), AllowedIPs, least privilege and default deny, the issue, review and revoke cycle.
